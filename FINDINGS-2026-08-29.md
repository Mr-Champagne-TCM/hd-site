# First real walk-through of the live site

Jeremy bought the summary, upgraded to the chart, and tried to upgrade to the
reading, on production with Stripe in sandbox. Everything he found, in his
order, with what I have since traced.

**STATUS: all of it is fixed and deployed, except the three noted at the bottom.**
See CHANGED-OVERNIGHT.md for what to check.

---

## Blockers — a tier-2 purchase takes money and delivers nothing

**B-1. The engine key is capped at tier 1.**
`This key reaches tier 1. Tier 2 was asked for.` The key is provisioned in
`hd-engine/.github/workflows/deploy.yml` as `ENGINE_KEYS=${ENGINE_KEY}:1:5000`
— the `1` is a tier ceiling. Tier 2 chart requests are refused by the engine.
One character, plus an engine deploy. **Jeremy has a paid tier-2 purchase
stuck behind this right now.**

**B-2. The upgrade charged FULL price** — the top tier's whole cost, with no
credit for the chart he had already bought.
Credit is read from a `grant` held in the browser tab that paid. He upgraded
from a page reached by an **email link on a later visit** — no grant in that
tab — so the credit was zero.

This is not an edge case. It is the path we now tell everyone to use, and the
PDF says in as many words: *"It is offered on the page your chart came from —
the one the link in your email opens — and what you have already paid comes off
the price there."* **That promise is currently false.**

Fix: the upgrade must send the reading TOKEN, and the credit must be derived
from the signed token's tier. Same strength as the grant — server-verified,
unforgeable — and it works from any device, any day.

## The upgrade funnel — the largest gap

**F-1. No upgrade offer on the output page reached straight from payment.**
It appears only when returning via the email link. Same page, two states, one
of them silently missing the funnel. Should be on both.

**F-2. The upgrade button goes straight to Stripe.** No chance to see what the
other tiers are or choose a different one.

Jeremy's design: clicking the upgrade returns to a version of the offer page
with **the discount already applied to the tiles**, the full price struck
through beside it, tiers already owned greyed out with "you already have this",
and only then through to Stripe with the discount fully applied.

**F-3. A teaser bodygraph** on the summary page, clearly labelled as NOT theirs.

**F-4. Where the "upgrade through your email link" instruction belongs.** It is
currently only in the PDF. He met it there first, which is the wrong place to
meet it. Needs a decision about where it goes and where it must not.

## Copy

**C-1. "Free reading, either way"** on the offer page reads as *a free Human
Design reading*. It means something else.

**C-2. Tier-0 email carries chart-tier content that is not what they bought.**
It should say what they DID buy, then describe the upgrade as a separate offer
rather than blurring the two. And ask whether anything is unclear.

**C-3. Tier-1 email, same shape.** The subject line is right ("Your Human
Design chart is ready") but the upgrade paragraph still reads as though it
describes what they are holding.

**C-4. "The same link has it"** — same link as what? Ambiguous in an email.

**C-5. "This link is active for six days."** Still true (D-9d: six days for the
link, a year for the purchase), but he asked, which means it reads as a threat
rather than as a fact.

## The form and the flow

**M-1. Birth details are re-entered on every upgrade.** Real friction, and it
carries a real hazard: enter them differently the second time and you silently
get a DIFFERENT chart. His thinking, kept whole:

> if we don't save birth details, and they enter a different birth dataset,
> they will get a different reading silently. big deal... not really, but one
> day someone will compare... they may need to correct birth data from an error
> last time OR they learned their birth time since the first entry... WE COULD
> compare chart output from 2nd entry to first and sound out if there is a diff
> — and if we do that we HAVE to be impeccable, bc doing this in error will
> lose client confidence greatly.

**M-2. The submit button says "My summary" at every tier.** At tier 1 they are
getting a chart; at tier 2, a reading.

**M-3. The "birth details are discarded" line is at the BOTTOM of the form.**
Should be at the top, or at least above the fields.

**M-4. After payment the chart page lands mid-page**, scrolled to the bodygraph.
The title is worth seeing. Landing from the email link is correct — top.

**M-5. Birth details are shown at the top of the output page** while the page
says they are discarded. He leans towards leaving it — they see their data, the
chart and the message together — pending M-1.

**M-6. Arrow-key navigation in the birth-place search.** Still wanted (Q-9,
deferred once already). Would not remove focus from the typing field.

## The bodygraph viewer

**Z-1. It opens far too close and cannot be zoomed out from there.** The
minimum scale fills the WIDTH, and the drawing is taller than it is wide, so on
a wide screen "fits width" means the top and bottom are already off screen with
nowhere to go. The floor should CONTAIN the whole chart, not fill the width.

**Z-2. No side-to-side movement** at that level, for the same reason.

**Z-3. Mouse wheel, the buttons and click-drag all work.** As described.

**Z-4. Pixel noise at maximum zoom.** Queued, not urgent. The PDF zooms
losslessly, which suggests a rendering difference in the viewer rather than in
the drawing.

## Delivery

**D-1. "Email me this link", from a page reached by that very link.** The
feature was designed when the email WAS the delivery. Now the link opens the
page and everything is on it. Worth asking whether it earns its place.

**D-2. Clicking it sent a duplicate** that threaded with the original. Working
as built, and it looks like a mistake.
